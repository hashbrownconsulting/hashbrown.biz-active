When a finance team considers new software, a security review usually runs alongside the demo. If you sell to finance, it helps to expect it. Vendors who prepare for it tend to move faster than those who treat it as a surprise.

This is a general guide, not compliance advice. Requirements differ by company, country and regulator.

## What reviewers usually ask

Common questions cover where data is stored and processed, who at the vendor can access it, how access is controlled, what is encrypted and how, what happens in an incident and how long data is kept. They also ask which certifications or independent audits you hold, how uptime is handled and what the exit process looks like.

## Answer early, in writing

A short, accurate page or document that covers those questions saves a round of email. Link to it from outreach or from your site. Where an answer depends on the plan or contract, say so plainly. Never claim a certification you do not hold, and if one is still in progress, say that.

## Name who owns it

Reviewers want to know who at your company they can talk to about security. Name a person or a role, and make it easy to reach them. An anonymous form feels like a gap.

## Do not oversell

Avoid phrases such as "bank-grade security" or "fully compliant" unless you can state what they mean and prove them. Specific facts, such as where data is hosted and how access is logged, are more convincing.

## Help your champion

The person who likes your product is often not the person who approves it. Give them a short summary they can forward to security, legal and finance, covering what the product does and how it handles data.
