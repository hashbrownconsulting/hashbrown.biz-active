A sales discovery call is a first conversation where the seller's job is to find out whether the buyer has a problem worth solving and whether the seller can solve it. It is not a pitch. The best ones end with either a clear next step or an honest agreement that it is not a fit, and both outcomes save everyone time.

## Before the call: decide what a good outcome is

Most discovery calls go wrong before they start, because the seller has no definition of success beyond "it went well." Decide in advance what you need to learn and what would make you stop. For a B2B sale with a meaningful ticket size, that is usually four things: whether the problem is real, whether it costs them enough to act on, who else is involved in deciding, and whether the timing is live or theoretical.

Read what is public about their business first, but do not use the call to recite it back to them. Research is for asking better questions, not for proving you did the research.

## The questions that do the work

Ask about the problem before anything else. What is happening today, how long has it been happening, and what have they already tried. A problem they have never tried to fix is usually not a problem they will pay to fix.

Then ask what it costs. Lost deals, wasted hours, slower growth, a founder stuck in every conversation. If they cannot put any weight on it, the priority is lower than the conversation suggested.

Then ask about the decision. Who else needs to be in the room, what has to be true for them to say yes, and what happened the last time they bought something like this. The last question is the most revealing one on the list, because it tells you how they decide and what burned them before.

Then ask about timing. Not "when do you want to start" but what happens if nothing changes this quarter.

> If a prospect cannot say what the problem costs, the call has found out something important: it is not urgent yet.

## How to end it

Every discovery call should end with one of two things: a next step with a date on it, or a plain statement that this is not the right fit. Anything in between, such as "let me send some information over," is a polite way of letting the lead die slowly.

If it is a fit, name the next step and put a time on it before you hang up. If it is not, say so and, where you can, point them to something that would help. It costs you a few minutes and earns more goodwill than a long tail of follow-ups to someone who was never going to buy.
