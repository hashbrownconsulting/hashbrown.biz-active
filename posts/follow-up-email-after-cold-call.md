After a cold call, send a short email within the hour that says who you are, references one specific thing from the call, and asks for one small next step. If they did not pick up, send it anyway and say so. Then follow up a limited number of times on a schedule, not on impulse.

## If they picked up: send it within the hour

The call is the warmest this contact will ever be, and it cools quickly. Write while they still remember your name. Keep it to a few lines. Open with the one thing that was actually said, whether that was a problem they named, a question they asked, or a timing they gave you, because a specific detail is the only thing that separates this email from a template.

Make one ask, and make it small. "Would Thursday at ten work for twenty minutes?" is an ask. "Let me know your thoughts" is not. Skip the deck and the attachment. A cold contact who has to open a file to understand why you emailed will not open it.

## If they did not pick up: say so

Do not write "great speaking with you." You did not speak, they know it, and the first line of the email has just cost you their trust. Say you tried to reach them, give one sentence on why you called, and make the same single small ask. Keep this version shorter than the one for people who answered, because you have less to reference and no relationship to lean on.

> A follow-up earns the next one by adding something. Repeating the ask does not.

## How many times to follow up, and how far apart

A short sequence beats an open-ended one. Three or four touches over about two weeks is a sensible shape, with the gap widening each time. More than that stops being follow-up and starts being pressure, which is the point where a mild lack of interest turns into a spam report.

Each touch should carry something the last one did not. The first references the call and asks. The second adds a relevant piece of information, such as a short example or the answer to a question they are likely to have. The third offers an easy way out: ask whether you should close the loop. People reply to that email more often than any of the others, because it is the only one that takes pressure off them.

## What changes when this runs at volume

One email is easy to get right. Running it across hundreds of contacts means tracking who answered, who did not, who has had which touch, and who has already said no.
