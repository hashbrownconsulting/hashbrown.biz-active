A solar and battery project is a mid-ticket decision, often anywhere from $10,000 to $500,000 depending on the site. Buyers at that level do not decide on a first email. They ask a predictable set of questions, and the supplier who answers them clearly is usually the one who gets the next conversation.

## How long until it pays back

This is almost always the first question, and it is the hardest to answer honestly. Payback depends on the site's usage, roof, tariff, system size and incentives. A fair answer explains what it depends on and offers to estimate it after a site review. A fixed number in a first email is a guess, and buyers know it.

## What is covered if something goes wrong

Buyers ask about warranties on panels, inverters and batteries, who handles claims and who services the system. Have the answer ready in plain language: what is covered, for how long and who they call. If part of it is the manufacturer's responsibility, say so.

## What the install involves

Operations and facilities teams want to know about downtime, access, approvals and timelines. A short description of the process, from site review to commissioning, reduces uncertainty. Do not promise dates you cannot control.

## How it is paid for

At this price level, finance is involved. Be clear about the options you offer, such as upfront purchase or finance through a partner, and what the buyer needs to provide. Leave advice on tax and incentives to qualified advisers.

## Who else needs to agree

An owner, a board or a landlord may have to approve. Give your contact something short and accurate to forward, covering what the system is, what it costs, what the first step is and what happens next.
