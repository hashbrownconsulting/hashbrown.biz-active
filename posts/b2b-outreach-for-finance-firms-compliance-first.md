This is a note on marketing practice, not legal or financial advice. Rules about financial promotion differ by country and change, so anything you send should be approved by your compliance lead or legal adviser.

With that said, the outreach that works best between finance businesses is usually the plainest. Most of what to leave out is also what busy buyers ignore.

## Write about the product, not the promise

A finance buyer wants to know what the product does, what it connects to and how it is secured. Describe those things in two or three sentences. Leave out claims about returns, guaranteed outcomes or being the safest option. They draw regulatory attention and make a professional buyer doubt the rest.

## Stay on the business side

Writing to a finance team about software, to a company about a lending product or to a firm about advisory services is business-to-business. Writing to individuals about their personal money is a different regime with stricter rules. If you are unsure which side a message falls on, ask your adviser before it sends.

## Be careful with proof

Customer names, testimonials and results are often restricted in financial services, and a number that is out of date can be misleading. Use only what you can document and what your compliance lead has approved. If you have no approved proof, a short product walkthrough is a safer substitute.

## Answer the security questions early

Finance buyers ask about data handling, access controls, certifications and uptime. If you have clear answers, put them on a page and link to it. It saves a round of email and builds trust.

## Ask for something small

A short call to see if the product fits their setup beats a request for a demo this week. The first message is there to earn a reply. The general method is in our [cold outreach guide](/blog/cold-outreach-guide/).

## Plan for a committee

Several people often weigh in, and a security review can add time. Follow up with something new each time rather than repeating the first message. See [lead follow-up: how fast, how often, when to stop](/blog/lead-follow-up/).
