Most outbound doesn't fail because the copy was weak. It fails because nobody ever saw it.

You can write the sharpest email of your life. If it lands in spam, or worse, if it never lands at all because the domain was cold and the mailbox provider quietly binned it, the copy was never the variable. The infrastructure was.

## The unglamorous eight weeks

New domains and new inboxes have no reputation. Send from them at volume on day one and the major providers treat you like exactly what you look like: a stranger sending a lot of mail. So you warm up. Small volumes, real-looking activity, gradual ramp, roughly eight weeks before you're sending anything that matters.

Nobody wants to hear "eight weeks." It's the least exciting slide in the deck. It's also the one that decides whether the other six work.

> Anyone promising qualified pipeline in two weeks is selling you a burned domain.

## What actually moves deliverability

A few things do most of the work, and none of them are clever:

- **Separate sending infrastructure.** Your main domain never takes the risk. You send from dedicated domains built for the job.
- **Authentication done properly.** SPF, DKIM, DMARC, set once, correctly, not "mostly."
- **A real warm-up ramp.** Volume that climbs on a schedule, not a switch you flip.
- **List hygiene.** Verified contacts only. One bad bounce rate spike undoes weeks of warm-up.

Get those right and your open rates stop being a mystery. Get one wrong and you'll spend months blaming the copy.
