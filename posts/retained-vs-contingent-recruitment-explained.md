Many hiring managers do not know the difference between retained and contingent recruitment, and some have had a bad experience with one of them. If you cannot explain it in two sentences, the conversation stalls before it reaches the role.

## The plain version

In a contingent arrangement, the agency is paid when a candidate it introduced is hired. The client usually works with several agencies on the same role, and only the one that places the candidate is paid.

In a retained arrangement, the agency is engaged for a role and paid in stages, commonly at agreement, at shortlist and at placement. The client usually works with that agency on that role, and the agency commits time to it.

The exact terms differ between agencies, regions and clients, so describe how you work rather than how "the industry" works.

## When each tends to fit

Contingent tends to suit roles that are common and where the client wants breadth and speed. Retained tends to suit senior, specialist or confidential roles where the search takes real effort and the client wants one team accountable. A role that is hard to fill can be a poor fit for contingent, because agencies may spend time elsewhere.

## What clients worry about

Contingent clients may worry about candidate quality or being flooded with profiles. Retained clients may worry about paying before a hire is made. Address the worry directly: say what they will receive and when, and what happens if the search does not fill the role.

## What to say in outreach

You do not need to explain the whole model in a cold message. Name the role type you place and the way you work in one sentence, such as "we run retained searches for senior sales roles". Then ask for a short call to hear the brief. The full approach to the first message is in [how recruitment agencies win new hiring clients](/blog/how-recruitment-agencies-win-hiring-clients/).

## Do not promise what you cannot control

Avoid promising speed, quality or a hire. A clear process and honest timelines build more trust. The call itself is covered in [what is a sales discovery call](/blog/what-is-a-sales-discovery-call/).
