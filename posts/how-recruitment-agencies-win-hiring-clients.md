Hiring managers and HR leads receive agency outreach every week, and most of it reads the same: a promise of great candidates and a request for a call. Agencies that win new clients tend to do something simpler.

## Pick a role type and say it

"We recruit across all industries" is hard to remember. "We place sales and customer success leads for B2B software teams" is easy to remember and easy to forward. A narrow focus signals that you know the market, even if you also take other work. Write the list around that focus.

## Show how you work

Clients want to know what happens after they say yes. In two lines, describe your process: how you take the brief, when they see a shortlist and how the fee works if you are willing to share it. Concrete beats clever. Do not invent timelines you do not meet.

## Time it to a vacancy

The best moment to reach a hiring manager is when a role is open or about to be. Job posts, funding news and team changes can all hint at that. Use them to decide who to write to first, and mention the trigger if it is public and relevant. Do not pretend a relationship you do not have.

## Ask for a small step

"Can we get on a call this week" is a big ask. A better first step is a short call to hear the brief for one role, or an offer to send two example profiles with names removed. The goal of the first message is a reply.

## Keep claims honest

Skip claims about placement speed, candidate quality or fees that you cannot support. Hiring managers talk to each other, and one bad experience travels.

## Follow up with something useful

A reply often comes on the second or third message. Add something each time, such as a market note on that role type, rather than "just checking in".
