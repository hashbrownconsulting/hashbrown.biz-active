If you sell to businesses in crypto and web3, your buyers are companies, not traders. That changes how you build a list, who you write to and what you say. This is a note on marketing practice, not advice on any financial product.

## Segment by what the company buys

"Crypto companies" is not a segment. An exchange, a protocol team, a custody provider, a payments firm and a fund all buy different things for different reasons. Pick the kind of company that has the problem you solve and write the list around it.

A segment is useful when you can describe it in a sentence: for example, teams that run validators and need monitoring, or payments firms that need compliance tooling.

## Find the right role

The buyer is usually not the founder. For infrastructure and tooling, it is often someone in engineering, operations or security. For compliance and payments products, it is often a compliance or finance lead. Check the role against what you sell. A well written message to the wrong person is still wasted.

## Use public information carefully

Public documentation, repositories, governance forums, job posts and conference lists can show which teams are active and what they use. Use them to qualify a company before you write, and to find one true detail to mention. Do not scrape personal information in ways that break the rules of a platform or privacy law. Check what applies in each country you target.

## Keep it to business contacts

Write to people in their professional role, at a company, about a business problem. Avoid messaging individuals about trading or investment, and avoid anything that reads as promotion of a token or product to the public.

## Test one segment first

Send to a small, clean segment, read the replies and learn what the buyers actually say. Widen only when the first segment tells you something. This also keeps domain reputation healthy, which matters for deliverability. See [why most outbound fails before the first email sends](/blog/hello-outbound/).

## Track what happens next

Record replies, calls and quotes against the segment, so you can tell which kind of company is worth more effort. See [five layers of a B2B outbound system](/blog/five-layers-b2b-outbound-system/) for how the pieces fit together.
