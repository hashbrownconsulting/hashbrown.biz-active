A commercial property owner with space to fill, or a lease coming up, usually talks to more than one agent before choosing. What they weigh is less about a pitch and more about whether the agent seems to understand the site and will keep them informed.

This is a general picture. Practices and rules differ by place.

## Local knowledge comes first

Owners want to know that the agent understands the area, the type of building and who is looking for space. Specifics help: the part of the market you work in, the kinds of tenants you speak to and what usually matters to them. Vague claims about "market expertise" do not.

## A clear process

Owners ask what happens after they say yes: how the space is presented, how enquiries are handled, how often they hear from the agent and what decisions stay with them. A short written plan for one site, before any agreement, is a concrete way to show this.

## Communication and trust

Many owners say they have been let down by agents who went quiet. Say how you report and how often. Name the person they will deal with. If you cannot promise a timeline, do not.

## Fees and terms

Owners usually want to understand fees, agreement length and exclusivity early. Be ready to explain your terms plainly. Rules on fees and advertising differ by place, so check them with your own adviser.

## What to avoid

Avoid claims about deals completed, prices achieved or yields unless you can document them and are allowed to use them. A sceptical owner will check.

## What this means for outreach

Name an asset type and an area, describe how you work in a sentence or two, and offer a plain first step such as a short written plan for a specific site. See [winning property owners with cold email](/blog/winning-property-owners-with-cold-email/) for the writing side.
