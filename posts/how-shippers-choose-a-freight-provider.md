Most shippers do not go looking for a new freight provider on a whim. A choice usually follows a trigger: a late shipment, a price rise, a new lane or a contract coming up for review. Understanding the trigger tells you when and how to appear.

This is a general picture, not a study. Every buyer is different.

## It starts with a reason

A problem with the current provider, or a change in what the business ships, opens the door. That is why the best moment to reach a buyer is often unpredictable. A steady, low-pressure presence, such as a short sequence that adds something each time, is more likely to land when the trigger happens than a single message.

## What they check

Buyers compare a short list of things: reliability and transit times on the lanes they use, price against what they pay now, how quickly someone answers, who handles customs and paperwork, and how easy it is to start. Few switch everything at once. Many try a provider on one lane first.

## Who is involved

Often a procurement or supply chain lead, with operations and finance weighing in. Operations cares about problems and delays. Finance cares about cost and invoicing. A message that makes it easy to forward to the right person helps.

## Why the low-risk first step wins

Switching has a cost: onboarding, new contacts, a learning period. A provider that offers a small trial, such as one lane or one shipment type, removes most of it. Say what the trial involves in plain terms.

## What this means for outreach

Name a lane or a service you really run, say that you would like to be considered as a second option, and ask for a short call to compare pricing. Leave out claims you cannot back up.
