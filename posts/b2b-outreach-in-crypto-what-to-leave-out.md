This is a note on marketing practice, not legal advice. Rules about crypto promotion differ by country and change often, so anything you send should be checked by your compliance lead or legal adviser.

With that said, there is a practical point: the outreach that works best in business-to-business crypto is the plainest. Most of what to leave out is also what makes buyers ignore you.

## Leave out returns, yields and price talk

Anything that sounds like a promise about money does two things. It draws regulatory attention in many places, and it makes a professional buyer assume you are selling something other than infrastructure. If you sell monitoring, custody tooling, compliance software or payments plumbing, talk about what the product does, not what it might earn anyone.

## Leave out retail language

Business buyers do not respond to language aimed at retail audiences, such as hype about the next big thing. Write for the person at a company who has a job to do: a head of operations, a compliance lead, an engineer. Name the problem, the product and a small next step.

## Leave out claims you cannot document

Technical buyers check. If you claim support for a network, an integration or a certification, make sure the documentation shows it. A single claim that does not hold up costs you the whole thread.

## Plan around platform rules

Ad platforms restrict or require approval for crypto and financial advertising, and the rules depend on the country. That is one reason outbound and organic search often carry more of the load in this sector than paid ads. Check the current policy for each platform before planning spend.

## Keep the ask small

As elsewhere, the first message is there to earn a reply. A short call to compare options, or a link to documentation, beats a request for a demo or a meeting this week.

## What to put in

Put in the product, the use case, who it is for and one fact the reader can verify. Link to a page that answers the obvious questions. Make it easy to forward. The general method is in our [cold outreach guide](/blog/cold-outreach-guide/).
