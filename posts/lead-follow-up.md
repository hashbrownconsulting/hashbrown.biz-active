Follow up with a new lead the same day. Give every lead one owner and a dated next step. Then run a short, defined sequence and stop when it ends. Lead follow-up fails less often because people are lazy than because it depends on someone remembering.

## How fast

As fast as you reasonably can, and the same day at the latest. A lead is warmest at the moment they raised their hand or replied, and that interest cools while you wait. The first response does not need to be long or polished. It needs to arrive while they still remember why they engaged.

Speed is also the cheapest advantage available. It costs nothing but process, and most competitors lose it to a shared inbox no one owns.

## Who owns it

Every lead should have exactly one person responsible for the next move, and a date on which that move happens. "The team is on it" means nobody is. When a lead has an owner and a next-step date, a stalled conversation becomes visible within days. When it does not, it becomes visible when someone asks why the pipeline looks thin.

> A lead with no owner and no next-step date is not in your pipeline. It is in your inbox.

## How often

A short sequence beats an open-ended chase. A few touches, spaced further apart each time, each carrying something the last one did not: a relevant example, an answer to a question they are likely to have, a plain offer to close the loop. If you want the shape in detail, we describe it for the cold-call case in [the follow-up email after a cold call](/blog/follow-up-email-after-cold-call/) and for booked calls in [what to put in a follow-up email after a sales call](/blog/follow-up-email-after-sales-call/).

Vary the channel when you can. An email that went unanswered may get a reply on LinkedIn, and the reverse is also true.

## When to stop

When the sequence is finished, stop, and record why. A lead that never replied goes into a different state from a lead that said no and a lead that said not now. Those three need different handling later, and lumping them together is how you end up emailing someone who already declined.

Stopping also protects your sending reputation. Persistent contact after silence is where a mild lack of interest turns into a spam report.

## The part that needs a system

One lead is easy. Hundreds across email and LinkedIn, at different stages, with different owners, is not something anyone tracks reliably by memory. That is the reply handling layer of an outbound system: replies routed to the right person, every lead in a defined state, every next step dated. Qualified conversations go to your sales team, and everyone else is handled by the sequence rather than by someone's good intentions.
